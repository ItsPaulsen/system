Designing pages the CMS can compose

A reading page is a stack of blocks a customer will rearrange. When the spacing lives in the flow rather than each block, any order still looks considered.

Ada Nolan 23 Jul, 2026

Editors do not think in pages, they think in blocks: a paragraph, then an image, then a table. The layout's job is to make every arrangement of those blocks read as one considered page, without anyone tuning margins by hand.

How do I get started?

Drop blocks into the flow in the order you want them read. Spacing is applied by the container, so nothing needs adjusting per block.

Can I change the colours?

Every colour derives from an anchor, so retinting is a one-line change.

Does it support dark mode?

Dark mode is a token swap. The page never redraws, so both themes stay in step.

Composing blocks, not pages

Treat the body as a single flow and let adjacent blocks decide the gap between them. Headings bind tightly to what follows; a list stays close to the sentence that introduces it; everything else gets the standard rhythm.

  1. Author adds a heading, then a paragraph, then a list.
  2. The flow sets 48 between blocks, 12 under a heading.
  3. The result reads correctly with no manual spacing.

Stop tuning margins block by block. Let the flow own the spacing, and any order still reads as one considered page.

Ada Nolan, Design systems lead

Spacing that survives a CMS

Because the gaps live in three rules rather than on each block, a customer can reorder the page freely and never break the rhythm.

  • Blocks default to a generous, even gap.
  • Coupled elements (heading to body, lead to list) sit closer.
Relationship Gap
Block to block 48
Heading to its block 12
Paragraph to its list 12
Title to standfirst 24
Body to related 96

The measure stays fixed and centred, so line length holds no matter how wide the screen gets. Media and text share that column; only the cover and the related grid break out to full width.
